# Dietary Pattern Limits and Fiber (Dietary Guidelines for Americans, 2020-2025)

## Official quantitative limits
The Dietary Guidelines for Americans, 2020-2025 state that a healthy dietary pattern doesn't have much room for extra added sugars, saturated fat, sodium, or alcoholic beverages. Official limits (starting at age 2, unless noted):

- **Added sugars** — Less than 10 percent of calories per day starting at age 2. Avoid foods/beverages with added sugars for children younger than age 2.
- **Saturated fat** — Less than 10 percent of calories per day starting at age 2.
- **Sodium** — Less than 2,300 milligrams per day — even less for children younger than age 14.
- **Alcoholic beverages** — Adults of legal drinking age can choose not to drink, or to drink in moderation: up to 2 drinks or less in a day for men and 1 drink or less in a day for women, when alcohol is consumed. Drinking less is better for health than drinking more. Some adults should not drink alcohol at all, such as those who are pregnant.

*Source: Dietary Guidelines for Americans, 2020-2025, USDA/HHS.*

## Fiber
The 2020-2025 Dietary Guidelines do not set a single numeric fiber target as a standalone headline recommendation; instead, they recommend meeting fiber needs through a healthy dietary pattern built from fiber-rich, nutrient-dense foods such as whole grains, vegetables, fruits, and legumes (consistent with the WHO recommendation to replace saturated fat with "carbohydrates from foods containing naturally occurring dietary fibre, such as whole grains, vegetables, fruits and pulses").

Example nutrient-dense, fiber-containing food sources highlighted by the Dietary Guidelines include legumes, whole grains, and a variety of vegetables and fruits, each providing meaningful amounts of dietary fiber per standard portion (the online materials define a "standard portion" as one providing at least 2.8 g of dietary fiber).

*Source: Dietary Guidelines for Americans — Food Sources of Dietary Fiber, dietaryguidelines.gov.*

## Relevance to triglycerides
These four levers — limiting added sugar, limiting saturated fat, limiting alcohol, and increasing fiber-rich whole foods — form the core, government-recommended dietary pattern associated with healthier triglyceride and overall cardiometabolic profiles, consistent with WHO and AHA guidance covered elsewhere in this knowledge base.
