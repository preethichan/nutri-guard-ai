# Saturated Fatty Acid and Trans-Fatty Acid Intake for Adults and Children (WHO, 2023)

This WHO guideline provides updated, evidence-informed guidance on saturated fatty acid (SFA) and trans-fatty acid (TFA) intake to reduce the risk of diet-related noncommunicable diseases, particularly cardiovascular disease. It replaces previous WHO guidance from 1989 and 2002.

## SFA (saturated fatty acid) recommendations
1. WHO recommends that adults and children reduce saturated fatty acid intake to **10% of total energy intake** (strong recommendation).
2. WHO suggests further reducing saturated fatty acid intake to **less than 10%** of total energy intake (conditional recommendation).
3. WHO recommends replacing saturated fatty acids in the diet with:
   - Polyunsaturated fatty acids (strong recommendation), or
   - Monounsaturated fatty acids from plant sources (conditional recommendation), or
   - Carbohydrates from foods containing naturally occurring dietary fibre, such as whole grains, vegetables, fruits, and pulses (conditional recommendation)

## TFA (trans-fatty acid) recommendations
1. WHO recommends that adults and children reduce trans-fatty acid intake to **1% of total energy intake** (strong recommendation).
2. WHO suggests further reducing trans-fatty acid intake to **less than 1%** of total energy intake (conditional recommendation).
3. WHO recommends replacing trans-fatty acids in the diet with polyunsaturated or monounsaturated fatty acids, primarily from plant sources (conditional recommendation).

## Context
These recommendations should be considered alongside other WHO guidelines on healthy diets, including those on total fat, polyunsaturated fatty acids, sugars, sodium, potassium, and carbohydrates. Because there were no relevant studies identified in a systematic review of TFA intake specifically in children, the pediatric TFA recommendations are extrapolated from adult study results.

*Source: WHO. Saturated fatty acid and trans-fatty acid intake for adults and children: WHO guideline. Geneva: World Health Organization; 2023. ISBN 978-92-4-007363-0.*

## Relevance to triglycerides
High intake of saturated and trans fats is associated with unfavorable blood lipid profiles. Switching from saturated fats toward unsaturated fats and fiber-rich carbohydrate sources is one of the dietary strategies commonly recommended alongside sugar/alcohol reduction for managing triglyceride levels (see also: MedlinePlus treatment recommendations, which list "switching from saturated fats to healthier fats" as a triglyceride-lowering lifestyle change).
